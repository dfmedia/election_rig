# Election Rig — Editor Moderator Guide (18 min)

Nov 10, 2026 · @Karl

## Session at a glance

An 18-minute Zoom interview with one person who helped publish election results for the Nov 3, 2026 general election. The goal is to learn how results actually got onto pages and into print: what worked, what broke, who did each step and how people worked around problems.

The kickoff deck makes these interviews the first step of the rebuild. Its December gate is "workflow defined, platform decided," so findings are due by mid-December.

- **Who:** recruit across three groups, because most setup this cycle was done centrally, not in newsrooms.
  - **Newsroom editors and producers** who published results. Include sites on Election Rig and sites that used something else: AP embeds only, the SCNG tool, the Denver tool, Press Democrat's older tool or the Twin Cities local tool. Include Denver: the kickoff notes say its embed generator and county-by-county results beat company-wide engagement.
  - **Central setup staff** in Product, Audience and CRUX who imported AP races, built Collections and ran the Google Sheets imports for local races.
  - **Print editors** who used the agate exports.
- **Setup:** Zoom with cloud recording and transcript on. Ask the participant to have their tool open and be ready to share their screen. Add a note-taker if one is free.
- **Decisions this informs:** the workflow the rebuild must support, which steps newsrooms can take over without a ticket, why some sites didn't use Election Rig, what print needs, and a first estimate of how long setup takes today.
- **Not covered here:** the timed CRUX walkthrough of today's workflow, which sets the official time-saved baseline. Run it as a separate session.

**Coordinate with Diana's discovery meetings.** The kickoff notes have Diana and Kalani meeting newsroom power users for workflow feedback. Share one participant list and this guide so no one is asked twice.

**Before each session (5 min of prep)**

- Look up the participant's site in the "KG - Nov. 2026" tab of the [2025-26 Elections](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1x7uhjdZNAFvJeiE_sufHbs5ZAYo9HwY2An9ERMOkjHE/edit?usp=sharing) sheet. Note which tool they used for web and print, and open one of their results pages.
- In the questions, replace "your tool" with the name they use for it. If they used more than one, start with the one they used most.

## Opening (2 min)

Read close to verbatim, then wait for answers.

> Hi \[name\], I'm \[name\]. Thanks for making time. This will take about 18 minutes.
>
> We're starting to plan a rebuild of the tools we use to publish election results. Before anyone designs anything, we want to understand how election results work today for the people who do the work.
>
> There are no wrong answers, and I didn't build any of these tools, so you won't hurt my feelings. The messier the real story, the more useful it is. This is about the tool, not your work. Nothing you say goes to your manager with your name on it.
>
> You can skip anything or say "I don't know."
>
> Is it OK if I record so I can listen instead of scribbling? The recording stays with the research team. *\[Wait for a clear yes.\]*
>
> Any questions before we start?

## Warm-up (2 min)

**Q1. To start, tell me about your role during election season this year.**

- Probe: "What part of the results work landed on you?"
- Probe: "What did you use to get results onto your site or into print?"
- Listen for: which markets and races they covered; which tools (Election Rig, AP embeds, the SCNG tool, the Denver tool, their own charts); whether they set up races themselves or someone else did it for them.

## Election night walkthrough (7 min)

Ask to share screen before Q2: "If you have \[tool\] handy, could you share your screen as we go?" If they can't, ask them to describe each screen instead.

**Q2. Take me back to the weeks before Nov 3. Walk me through how you got your races ready, starting with the first thing you did.**

- Probe: "What did that look like, exactly?"
- Probe: "Who else did part of that?"
- Probe: "Was there a moment where that almost didn't work?"
- Probe: "Roughly how long did it take, from the first step to a chart on the page?"
- If they used a tool other than Election Rig, probe: "How did you end up using \[tool\] for results?"
- Listen for: waiting on someone else to import AP races; Collections built for them; the Oct 26 deadline for Collection IDs; tickets to the tech team; the Google Sheets import for local races; testing before election night.
- Pick two or three probes. Don't run all six.

**Q3. Now take me to election night. What were you doing when the first results started coming in?**

- Probe: "What were you keeping an eye on?"
- Probe: "What were you expecting to happen, and what actually happened?"
- Probe: "What happened next?"
- Listen for: results not refreshing, error pages, wrong or late data, changing an AP result or race call, not knowing what went wrong.

**Q4. Tell me about a race that didn't come in the way most of the others did.**

- Probe: "How did its results end up on the page?"
- Probe: "Who else was involved in getting it there?"
- Listen for: non-AP local races, hand-entered sheets, state-site data, late or missing races, maps.

## Workarounds and breakdowns (4 min)

**Q5. Tell me about something you did outside \[tool\] to get your results published.**

- Probe: "Walk me through the last time you did that."
- Probe: "How did you learn to do it that way?"
- Probe: "How long did it take that night?"
- Listen for: spreadsheets, manual edits in the CMS, screenshots, Flourish or other embeds, Slack requests to the tech team.

**Q5b (if time). Tell me about the last time you got stuck in \[tool\].**

- Probe: "Who did you reach out to?"
- Probe: "What happened while you waited?"

## Readers and output (2 min)

**Q6. Tell me about how you decided what readers would see on your results pages this cycle.**

- Probe: "Was there anything you wanted to show that you couldn't?"
- Probe: "How did results get into print?"
- Probe: "What did you hear from readers about the results pages?"
- Listen for: maps by district or county, charts, mobile layout, homepage widgets, the apps and app home-screen widgets, print agate files, the e-edition, voter guides.

## Wrap-up (1 min)

**Q7. What should I have asked about election night that I didn't?**

Then close:

> That's really helpful, thank you. One last thing: if you kept a checklist, spreadsheet or notes for election night, would you be willing to share a copy? \[Wait.\] And is it OK if we follow up with a quick question later?

## Moderator notes

**How to ask**

- One question at a time. Never stack two in one turn.
- Let silence sit for 3 to 5 seconds. The second answer is usually the real one.
- Stay neutral. If an editor vents, don't agree; say "Tell me more about that."
- Don't name features or suggest fixes. If they start designing, ask "What would that let you do that you can't do now?"
- If they generalize ("we always..."), bring it back: "Tell me about the last time that happened."
- Don't ask "Would you use...?" It produces guesses, not evidence.

**If you're running short, cut in this order**

1. Q5b
2. Q4's second probe
3. Q6's reader probe (keep the print probe for print editors)
4. The warm-up probes

Never cut Q3 or Q5. They carry the evidence the rebuild depends on.

**Probe bank**

- "Tell me more about that."
- "What did that look like, exactly?"
- "Walk me through the last time that happened."
- "What were you expecting to happen, and what actually happened?"
- "What made you say that?"

**Words participants may use**

Know these so you can follow along. Don't introduce them yourself; use the participant's words.

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| AP import | Pulling AP races into the rig by state and election date; done centrally this cycle |
| Collections | Groups of races with an order and headings; most were built centrally this cycle |
| Collection IDs | Numbers due by Oct 26 so developers could add them to the election-night refresh rules |
| Chart items | The charts and maps embedded in WordPress articles |
| Google Sheets import | The multi-step import for local races AP doesn't cover, run on a shared account |
| print1, print2, print3 | Agate files sent to FTP for print: race charts, county-by-county results and referendums |
| AP embeds | AP's own results iframes, used for maps and balance of power and by sites not on the rig |
| SCNG tool, Denver tool | Separate results tools used in Southern California and Colorado |
| Embed generator | Denver's way of making results embeds, including county-by-county results |
